Reflection

My notes from the audit trail, screenshots, and working database.

01

How I would rate the agents

Ticket 101: Boss, 4/5. Boss used shop_operations_context and customer_order_ticket_101, found that size S had zero listed units, and noticed invoice 501 for $840 was still open for Bulldog Print Co. I liked that the reply stayed a draft and did not promise a restock. I would take off a point because the Expected plan called for Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service, but the actual audit shows Boss handled it alone. The result was still clear and supported by the tools.

Ticket 102: Boss, 4/5. Boss checked the lease and cash context, used the desk date of 2026-08-31, and correctly compared the $2,400 rent with the $3,400 starting checking balance. The audit shows no Facilities or Accounting handoff, even though that was in Expected. I then approved the payment myself; the agent did not approve it. I liked that the recommendation left the decision to me.

Ticket 103: Boss, 4/5; Inventory, 5/5. Boss called the ticket and shop-context tools, then handed the stock/pricing question to Inventory. Inventory called both tools and returned the useful limits: 8 size-M units for a request of 20, no approved discount, and no hoodie vendor identified. Boss did not send the request to Accounting or Customer Service, which made sense because the records did not establish a supplier or an approved price. The answer avoided promising stock or a discount.

02

How Actual compared with Expected

101: Expected was Boss → Inventory → Accounting, then a Customer Service draft. Actual was Boss alone, calling shop_operations_context and customer_order_ticket_101. Boss still covered the stockout, linked unpaid invoice, and draft. The audit contains no extra agent handoffs.

102: Expected was Boss → Facilities → Accounting → Boss. Actual was Boss alone with shop_operations_context and rent_notice_ticket_102. After that run, I approved the $2,400 rent payment in the dashboard. Payment #1 records Stefan as approver, and checking now shows $1,000. The screenshot labels the ticket resolved in the board, but the ticket row in SQLite is still open.

103: Expected included Inventory and then Accounting and Customer Service if their checks were needed. Actual was Boss → Inventory → Boss. Boss used shop_operations_context and price_override_ticket_103; Inventory used price_override_ticket_103 and shop_operations_context. No Accounting or Customer Service handoff is in the audit. That narrower route was enough to show the 12-unit gap and avoid inventing a vendor or discount.

03

What was simpler with one agent and tools

Tickets 101 and 102 were simpler as one Boss agent with the right MCP tools. In the actual runs, Boss gathered the relevant ticket and shop context directly and returned a useful answer without an extra handoff. Ticket 102 especially had a short path: confirm the lease and balance, recommend human review, then let me approve the rent myself. Ticket 103 benefited from one handoff because Inventory separately checked the size-M quantity and pricing facts; even there, the audit shows no need to involve every specialist. I would keep delegation available, but use it only when another agent adds a real check.

04

Three more problems this team can help with

  1. If Tauhid asks what other tee sizes are listed, Boss or Customer Service can use customer_order_ticket_101 to see the recorded counts (M 5, L 3, XL 2) and draft an answer without saying the customer accepted a substitute.
  2. If I ask whether the recorded $840 invoice could fit within the current checking balance, Accounting can use shop_operations_context to compare $840 with the current $1,000 and prepare a human-review recommendation. That arithmetic leaves $160; it does not pay the invoice or clear its open status.
  3. If Yale AI Club asks for a fact-based update about the hoodie request, Inventory and Customer Service can use price_override_ticket_103 to explain that 8 size-M units are listed against 20 requested, and that the database lists $22 unit cost and $58 list price but no approved discount.

These are follow-up questions on the records and ticket tools we have. They do not mean the server can look up an arbitrary new ticket or make a commitment.

05

Three problems it cannot complete yet

  1. Place or reserve a restock order. The tools report listed stock and prices, but they do not reserve inventory, place a purchase order, or confirm supplier availability. I would need a product/vendor catalog plus order and reservation MCP tools. Inventory could prepare it, with Accounting checking costs and a person approving the purchase.
  2. Send a real customer or landlord message. The agents can write drafts, but there is no email or messaging tool. A secure contact/CRM MCP tool with recipient checks and a separate human send confirmation would be needed; Customer Service could prepare the message, but should not send it on its own.
  3. Resolve arbitrary tickets in the database. These ticket tools are specifically for IDs 101, 102, and 103, and the board’s resolved label is local to the browser. To find other tickets and persist a resolution, we would need a general ticket lookup plus an audited ticket-status update MCP tool. Boss and the relevant specialist should verify the outcome before a human-approved status change.
